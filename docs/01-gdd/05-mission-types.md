# 05 — Mission Types

Twelve levels, nine mission archetypes, one verb set. Each archetype is built from the same shape —
**SET UP → THE FIDDLY BIT → THE COMPLICATION → CLEAR UP** — but differs in what it asks of the
player's *attention*.

The design axis that keeps them distinct:

| Archetype | Primary pressure | Player's attention is on… |
|---|---|---|
| A. SURVEY | none (tutorial) | learning to read |
| B. CONDUCTOR | descent + weather clock | the route down |
| C. GILD | precision + wind | your own hands |
| D. BAND | lateral traversal + weight | going sideways, away from safety |
| E. TOP | rhythm + shrinking world | the structure dissolving under you |
| F. FELL | spatial planning + dread | a load diagram and a fall line |
| G. MECHANISM | puzzle + heavy objects | a machine, at height |
| H. LATTICE | 3D movement + heat | the void |
| I. EMERGENCY | time + storm | everything at once |

---

## A. SURVEY / INSPECT
**"Go up, look at it, tell me what's wrong, come down."**

- **Set up:** nothing. This is the training level's job.
- **Fiddly bit:** find and log `n` defects (cracked course, perished banding, a bird's nest in the
  flue, missing lightning clip). Each is found by *looking* and confirmed by *tapping*. You mark it
  with chalk.
- **Complication:** one defect is somewhere awkward — under the corbel, requiring an overhang move.
- **Clear up:** descend; the report screen shows what you found vs. what was there. Missing defects
  costs fee.
- **Why it's here:** it teaches reading, tapping, and the anchor loop with zero time pressure. Also
  makes an excellent low-stress replayable side job.

## B. CONDUCTOR
**"Run copper from the top to an earth pit at the bottom."**

- **Set up:** climb (normal ascent), fix the air terminal at the apex, and rig the tape reel.
- **Fiddly bit:** **descend while working.** You pay out copper tape and fix a clip every 1.5 m.
  Each clip is a small aim-and-drive action, done one-handed, on the way down. You cannot slide.
  The tape must stay taut and reasonably straight — a slack or wandering run fails inspection.
- **Complication:** the tape reel is finite and you *will* misjudge it if you wander. Also: the tape
  must route around a steel band, and the obvious way is wrong.
- **Clear up:** dig/connect the earth pit at the base; test with a continuity meter (a satisfying
  needle-and-buzz moment).
- **Why it's here:** it makes the descent — normally the boring bit — into the main event, and it's
  a natural home for a **weather clock** (a storm is coming; being on a chimney holding a copper
  cable in a storm is not ideal and the game finds this funny).

## C. GILD
**"The weathercock's seized and the gold's gone. Sort it."**

- **Set up:** spire climbing is different — you can't circle a spire on ladders, so you rig a
  **bosun's chair** from a ring lashed around the spire, and work suspended. Rigging it is a real
  multi-step sequence and is the level's first act.
- **Fiddly bit:** three-phase.
  1. **Release** — the weathervane's spindle is seized. Penetrating oil, wait, shock it with the
     hammer, unwind. Rounding the nut here is a genuine failure state with a workaround (cut it,
     costing time and a spare).
  2. **Lower** — send the weathercock down on a rope, controlled by friction. Too fast and it
     smashes on the roof (job failed, expensive). This is a pure **feel** minigame: hold the brake,
     modulate against gravity, watch it descend 45 m. Deeply tense and completely silent.
  3. **Re-gild and re-hang** — gilding verb (see tools), then haul back up, seat, and **plumb it**.
- **Complication:** wind, always. The chair spins. You are working on the single highest point for
  five miles with nothing to hold.
- **Clear up:** de-rig the chair; the vane must spin freely (test it — a lovely final beat: it turns,
  it points into the wind, the bell rings).
- **Why it's here:** the game's "precision at the apex" level. It's beautiful, it's quiet, and it's
  the one people will screenshot.

## D. BAND / REPAIR
**"She's cracked from the top down. Get three steel bands round her."**

- **Set up:** measure the circumference at three heights (tape verb, lateral traversal), order the
  bands (they're cut to your measurement — **measure badly and they don't fit**, which costs you a
  trip down and a chunk of fee).
- **Fiddly bit:** haul heavy steel segments (the gin wheel's hardest haul — 90 kg, swings badly),
  then work *around* the chimney bolting them together. Lateral re-dogging: you leave the safety of
  your vertical stack and crab sideways on two anchors. Then **tension** the band: tightening bolts
  in the correct star sequence, or it goes oval and won't seat.
- **Complication:** one of the three bands sits exactly where the brickwork is perished, so you can't
  anchor there — you must work that band from above, on a chair, hanging.
- **Clear up:** repoint the crack; strike ladders.
- **Why it's here:** introduces the "sideways is scarier than up" idea and heavy haulage.

## E. TOP (dismantle brick by brick)
**"Can't fell it — there's a chapel twenty feet off. She'll have to come down by hand."**

The one the reference material is most famous for. Fully specified in
[`07-topping-system.md`](07-topping-system.md). Summary:

- **Set up:** build a small timber staging around the top of the shaft, lashed to the stack.
- **Fiddly bit:** remove the coping/cap in sequence, then work the courses down, prising bricks and
  **dropping them down the inside of the flue**. Rhythm, resistance-feel, and a constant small
  judgement (save the brick, or snap it and go faster).
- **Complication:** the flue jams. The staging must be lowered as the chimney shrinks. **The level
  geometry actively changes under you** — your own ladder stack now sticks up above the chimney and
  must be struck as you descend. This is the single most novel thing in the game.
- **Clear up:** you're already at the bottom. Count the bricks; salvaged brick is worth money.
- **Why it's here:** the "world dissolves" fantasy. It's the level people will talk about.

## F. FELL
**"She's coming down. And she's going *that* way."**

Fully specified in [`06-felling-system.md`](06-felling-system.md). Summary:

- **Set up (act 1):** ground survey. Walk the site. Mark the fall line with pegs. Identify exclusion
  objects (the chapel, the gasholder, the railway, the pub). Check the chimney's existing lean with
  the plumb bob — **it has an opinion about where it wants to go and you must work with it.**
- **Set up (act 2):** climb and strip — remove the lightning conductor, the steel bands, and often
  the top 5–10 m by hand, because a shorter chimney falls more predictably. *The FELL levels reuse
  the TOP system as their second act.*
- **Fiddly bit (act 3):** **cut the gob.** Chisel out brickwork at the base in a precise arc,
  inserting timber props behind you as you go. Live load diagram. Escalating dread.
- **Complication:** authored per level — a prop splits, the mortar is stronger on one side, a
  spectator wanders into the exclusion zone.
- **Clear up (act 4):** pack with waste timber, fire it, **run**, and watch. Score on angular
  accuracy and collateral.
- **Why it's here:** it's the marquee. Everything else in the game is set-up for this.

## G. MECHANISM
**"The clock's stopped and the minute hand's on the floor of the belfry."**

- **Set up:** a tower, not a chimney — internal stairs and ladders get you most of the way, so the
  ascent is short. The *external* work is the hard part.
- **Fiddly bit:** a genuine puzzle-flavoured job — remove a hand (it's 2.4 m long and weighs 40 kg),
  lower it, straighten it, haul it, and re-seat it on a square taper **at the correct clock
  position**, which means you have to stop the clock, set it, and restart it. Meanwhile you're
  standing on a 40 cm ledge on the face of the tower.
- **Complication:** the clock strikes. A huge bell, right behind you, at close range. Nerve -35.
  (Foreshadowed by a visible bell and a visible time. Players who look will see it coming. Players
  who don't will jump out of their skin. This is the one jump-scare in the game and it's fair.)
- **Why it's here:** pacing. A low-altitude, high-fiddliness level to break up the tall stacks.

## H. LATTICE
**"Steel, not brick. And it's twice as tall."**

- **Set up:** no ladders. You climb the structure itself with a twin-lanyard leapfrog.
- **Fiddly bit:** replace corroded rivets/bolts on the structure — including **hot riveting**, which
  is a two-person job done solo with a jig, and is the game's most mechanical skill test.
- **Complication:** the lattice is a 3D maze; the route up is not obvious and dead-ends exist. Plus:
  it's a pleasure-beach tower, so it is *in use*, with people and noise and lights below you.
- **Clear up:** gild the ball at the top. Callback to Level 3, now at three times the height.
- **Why it's here:** to make the player miss the ladders, and to give the finale somewhere to build
  from.

## I. EMERGENCY
**"Storm took the top off it. It's over the school. Now."**

- No survey, no loadout planning — the game picks your kit and you start climbing.
- Rain: grip ×1.4, no sliding. Wind gusts on a timer with an audible tell (you hear it coming across
  the rooftops **1.2 s before it hits** — the single best "learn the tell" moment in the game).
- The job is a partial TOP under duress: remove a dangerously loose section before it goes.
- **Why it's here:** it's the difficulty spike, and it proves every system holds up under pressure.

## Mission type → level mapping

See [`../02-levels/level-index.md`](../02-levels/level-index.md).

Rules for sequencing:
1. **Never two of the same archetype back to back.**
2. Every archetype is taught in a low-stakes level before it is used in a high-stakes one.
3. FELL appears three times (L6 forgiving, L7 constrained, L12 finale) — the player must feel the
   growth in their own judgement, not in their tools.
4. The last five levels combine archetypes (e.g. L12 = TOP act + FELL act).
