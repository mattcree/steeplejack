## The one-line pitch
The level where the chimney you climbed gets shorter than your own ladders. Introduce hand
demolition in its purest form, with no felling and no time panic — just rhythm, craft, and a slowly
disappearing world.

## Briefing

> *Sir — the old bleach croft stack is leaning and the Borough Surveyor has served a notice. It
> cannot be dropped, there being a Methodist chapel not twenty feet off, and the chapel people are
> not reasonable men. The Surveyor says if the top sixty foot comes off the rest will stand.*
> *£540, and you may keep the brick.*
> *Municipal Borough of Crossley, Dept. of Works*

## Site layout

```
              ▓     ← 60m, leaning ~1.5° toward the NE (toward the chapel)
             ▓▓
            ▓▓▓
           ▓▓▓▓
      ╔═══╗▓▓▓▓
      ║   ║▓▓▓▓   ← Methodist chapel, 6m away NE. Slate roof. Untouchable.
      ║   ║▓▓▓▓
      ╚═══╝▓▓▓▓
        old bleach croft (ruin, open)      [van]
```

**Everything you drop over the side goes on the chapel.** This is why the flue exists as a mechanic:
the *only* safe place to put a brick is down the middle of the chimney.

## The work

### Set up — the staging (12 min)
Build a timber platform around the top of the shaft:
- 4 putlog dogs, driven at 90° around the circumference at 58 m (lateral traversal, now routine)
- 4 putlogs laid in
- 6 boards
- 8 lashings

**Then you step onto it.** The first time in the game you can walk around freely at height. Camera
pulls out. It is 1.2 m wide, there is a black hole in the middle, and it is sixty metres up. The
character has a cup of tea on it, because of course he does.

### The fiddly bit — the courses (35 min)
Remove **18 m** of chimney, by hand, one brick at a time, down the flue.

1. **The coping.** A corbelled oversail cap. Remove in the correct interlock order (a genuine
   ten-second spatial read). Wrong order → a slab peels, nerve −30, possible slip-save, and a
   chunk of it goes over the side toward the chapel.
2. **The courses.** ~20 interactive bricks per metre × 18 m ≈ **360 bricks**, at ~1.8 s each.
   Prise, judge the give, drop down the flue. A long, rhythmic, genuinely meditative stretch.
3. **Lower the staging every 2 m.** Nine times. Unlash, re-dog, re-lash, re-board. This is the
   metronome that keeps the coring from going numb.
4. **Strike your own ladders.** By 50 m the top of your stack is above the chimney and in the way.
   You dismantle the route you built, from the top, as you go.

### The complication
Three, spaced per the 4 m rule:
- **At ~14 m remaining: the flue jams.** The falling-brick sound changes from a long rush to a
  short clatter. Clear it by dropping a weight on a rope (inverted haul). Ignore it and the flue
  fills — then you must carry bricks down, which is miserable and slow and entirely your own fault.
- **At ~10 m remaining: a jackdaw's nest**, a metre of packed twigs. Pull it out. Birds explode
  out of the flue. Nerve −8 and a genuine jump.
- **At ~5 m remaining: a wrought-iron tie ring** cast into the brickwork. Cold chisel, 30 s, and it
  will not let go quietly.

### Clear up
You are already at 42 m, standing on what's left. Descend what remains of your stack. Count the
brick. Clear the flue base.

## Beats

1. Shimming a ladder on a leaning chimney and feeling the geometry fight you.
2. The old dogs at 30 m — taking the gamble, or not.
3. **Stepping onto the staging.** Free movement at 60 m. Tea on a scaffold board.
4. The first brick down the flue: a long, long descending rattle that ends in a distant thump.
   **This sound is the level's whole identity.** Get it right.
5. **HERO BEAT:** around the halfway point, the camera shows the player that the chimney is now
   *shorter than their own ladder stack*, which sticks up past the top like a mast. The character
   says one dry line about it. Never mentioned again.
6. The last brick of the day, and looking down into the flue at a pile of your own rubble, 42 m down.

## Failure modes available here

| Failure | Telegraph | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Coping peeled | the interlock is visible if you look | nerve −30, slip-save, chapel damage risk |
| Snapped brick | the give is audible | rubble instead of salvage; craftsmanship grade |
| Brick over the side | you can see where you're throwing | **chapel slate, −£30, reputation −3** |
| Flue full | the jam sound, ignored twice | carry-down mode; rate ×0.3 |
| Staging not lowered in time | you're reaching down too far; grip drains fast | forced to stop and lower it |
| Fall | standard | standard |
