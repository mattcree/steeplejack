## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Archetype** | GILD |
| **Height** | 45 m (tower 28 m + stone spire 17 m) |
| **Structure** | Gritstone church tower and octagonal broach spire |

## The one-line pitch
The beautiful one. Take the player to the highest, most exposed point they've seen, give them
nothing to hold onto, and ask them to do something delicate.

## Briefing

> *Dear Mr. Hartley,*
> *Our weathercock has not turned since the winter before last and the gilding is entirely gone —
> from the vicarage it looks like a crow. The Parochial Church Council has voted £420 for the work,
> which is more than we can strictly afford, so we would be grateful if it could be done properly.*
> *The scaffolders wanted £1,900 and six weeks.*
> *Yours in hope, Revd. T. Sowerby*

## Site layout

```
              ✦  ← weathercock, 45m
             ╱▲╲
            ╱   ╲    ← octagonal broach spire, 17m, gritstone
           ╱     ╲
          ┌───────┐
          │ ▤   ▤ │  ← tower, 28m, louvred belfry
          │       │
   ┌──────┤       ├──────┐
   │ nave │       │      │  ← lead roof at 11m (things landing here = £££)
   └──────┴───────┴──────┘
        graveyard, yews, [van on the lane]
```

**The lead nave roof at 11 m is the hazard.** Drop the weathercock and it lands there. So does
anything else.

## Ascent bands

| From | To | Band | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 28 m | **Internal** — tower stair + belfry ladder | free. The game gives you the tower; you emerge onto the parapet. A deliberate change of pace and a lovely reveal (dark stair → bright parapet). |
| 28 | 33 m | **Spire base / broaches** | stone, not brick. **Joints are 4× further apart** — you can't dog wherever you like, you must use the courses that exist. First real route-*finding*. |
| 33 | 41 m | **Tapering spire** | the ladder can't sit flush on a taper. **Shims/packing introduced.** Spans shorten as the spire narrows. |
| 41 | 45 m | **The finial** | no more ladder. You rig the chair from here. |

## The work

### Set up — rigging the chair (10 min, the level's first act)
1. Lash a **ring** (a rope collar) around the spire below the finial. Four wraps, tensioned.
2. Hang the **bosun's chair** from the ring on a two-part tackle.
3. Rig a **descent line** to the ground for the weathercock.
4. Sit in it, and let go of the ladder.

That last step is a deliberate, held input with a 1.5 s commitment animation. **The camera pulls out
as he sits back into nothing.** Nerve −20 on the spot, and then the drain rate *drops* because the
chair is the safest stance in the game. Terrifying, then calm. Perfect.

### The fiddly bit — three phases

**Phase 1: Release.**
The spindle nut is seized solid. Options, discoverable:
- Brute force → **rounds the nut** (permanent; forces the cut-off workaround, −12 min, −1 spare)
- Penetrating oil, wait 60 s, then turn → works, but costs a minute of shift
- Oil + a sharp hammer shock, then turn → works immediately. The expert solution.

The chair spins slowly as you work. You must **manage your own rotation** (a gentle push against the
spire) to stay facing the nut. It's small and it's brilliant.

**Phase 2: Lower.**
The weathercock (28 kg, 1.4 m of copper) goes down 45 m on a friction line.

- Pure feel. Hold the brake; modulate against gravity.
- Too fast → it accelerates; past a threshold you **can't recover**, and it hits the lead roof.
  Job failed. £420 gone, reputation −10, a very bad letter from the vicar.
- Too slow → it takes four minutes of your shift.
- Wind swings it. You steer with the line.
- **The camera looks straight down the line for the entire descent.** No music. Rope noise, wind,
  breathing. It takes 90 seconds if done well and it is the most tense 90 seconds in the first half
  of the game.

**Phase 3: Gild and re-hang.**
- Wire-brush, then **size** (coverage meter), then **leaf** (hold-breath windows, finite leaves,
  the wind will take one if you're careless), then **burnish**.
- Done on the ground — a rare, calm, safe interlude at a trestle in the graveyard with birds.
  **Use it. The contrast makes the next bit land.**
- Haul it back up. Heaviest haul so far, and it's shaped like a sail.
- Seat it on the spindle, grease it, pin it, and **plumb it** — it must be within 1° of vertical or
  it won't turn freely.

### The complication
**The wind rises through the level**, 8 → 13 m/s. Phase 3's haul, in the last third, is in the worst
wind of the day — with a large flat copper object on a rope. It will swing into the spire if you
haul greedily, and a dented weathercock costs you the "properly done" bonus.

### Clear up
De-rig the chair, coil everything, descend. **Final beat:** at the bottom, in the graveyard, look
up. The vane turns. It points into the wind. It catches the sun.

## Beats

1. The dark tower stair, the belfry, and stepping out onto the parapet into daylight.
2. Stone instead of brick — the player's whole anchor instinct is wrong for thirty seconds.
3. **Sitting back into the chair.** The hand lets go of the ladder.
4. **HERO BEAT:** the 90-second lower. Silence, a rope, 45 m, and a lead roof.
5. The trestle in the graveyard. Gold leaf, sunshine, birds, tea. The game's quietest minute.
6. From the ground: it turns.

## Failure modes available here

| Failure | Telegraph | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Rounded nut | resistance + a grinding sound before it goes | −12 min, use a spare |
| **Smashed weathercock** | the descent-rate meter goes red for 4 s before the point of no return | **JOB FAILED** |
| Lost gold leaf | wind gust tell + your own wobble | −£1.40 each, run out and you can't finish today |
| Dented vane on the haul | swing meter | lose the craftsmanship bonus |
| Vane out of plumb | the plumb bob, and it visibly doesn't turn | rework, −8 min |
| Fall from the chair | it's the safest stance — this should be rare and that's the point | standard |
