## Goal
Every nerve number that shapes the game is one a designer chose on purpose and wrote down.

## Why
It currently lists six; `meters.json` has nine, and METER-002 implements all nine. Rule 9 says one
of them is a bug. The data is almost certainly right — the task's own acceptance criterion says
"all nine" — but three shocks are in the game with no design rationale written down anywhere, and
one of them is the largest shock in the game.

## Context
In the data and not in the GDD table:

| key | value | in the table? |
|---|---|---|
| `copingPeel` | −30 | no |
| `bellStrike` | −35 | no |
| `sitIntoChair` | −20 | no |

For comparison, the largest shock that *is* documented is `anchorFail` at −30 — an anchor letting
go underneath you.

`sitIntoChair` at −20 is interesting in its own right: the bosun's chair is the *safest* stance in
the game (zero grip drain, both hands free), and getting into one costs the same nerve as a haul
load swinging into you. That may be exactly right — the moment of committing your weight to a rope
is the frightening part — but it is a real design statement that nothing records.

## Blocked

### Question 2: is nerve still the slow meter at the top of the range?

The GDD calls nerve "minutes-to-hours" against grip's "seconds-to-minutes", and METER-002's first
handoff note repeated that as "an order of magnitude". Measured against the shipped data, it is not:

| operating point | grip | nerve | |
|---|---|---|---|
| hanging at 110 m, 12 m/s wind, one-handed | 8.0/s | 1.34/s | 6.0× |
| at the height and wind caps, overhang | 8.0/s | 2.97/s | 2.7× |
| at the caps, **belted** | 1.0/s | 2.97/s | **nerve is 3× faster** |

Time from a fresh 90 to frozen: 600 s at 40 m on a platform in still air, 156 s at 110 m on a
ladder, 67 s hanging at 110 m in a 12 m/s wind, **30 s** at the caps.

So at a belted stance high on a stack — which is the stance the game *encourages*, costing 5 s to
rig and buying a hundred seconds of grip — nerve is the meter that runs out first, in half a minute.

That may be exactly the intent: rigging a belt makes your hands safe and leaves you hanging there
thinking about it, and "the safe stance is the frightening one" is a good mechanic. It may equally
be a number nobody multiplied out. Nothing in the docs distinguishes those.

This is the GDD's own formula over the GDD's own data, faithfully implemented — **there is no bug
here to fix.** The question is whether the design means it.

### Question 1: the three undocumented shocks

Is `bellStrike` at −35 meant to be the worst shock in the game, worse than an anchor failing under
you? And what are `copingPeel` and `sitIntoChair` modelling?

**What I tried.** Grepped the GDD, the level docs and the ADRs for all three names; they appear only
in `data/tuning/meters.json`. No design doc mentions a bell.

**Options.**

1. The data is right and the table is stale. Add the three rows with a line of rationale each.
2. The three were added speculatively and some should go. Removing one means METER-002's
   enumeration test drops to eight, which is a one-line change.
3. `bellStrike` is right but `anchorFail` is too low — the numbers are relative to each other and
   changing one is a balance decision.

**Recommendation: option 1.** METER-002's acceptance already says nine, the values are internally
plausible, and the cheapest fix is to write down what they mean. But *"is the bell worse than the
anchor"* is a design judgement and not an implementer's to make, which is why this is blocked
rather than ready.

## Acceptance
1. The GDD's shock table lists all nine shocks with their tuned values.
2. Each of the three undocumented shocks has a line saying what it models.
3. The relative ordering is deliberate and stated — in particular whether `bellStrike` outranking
   `anchorFail` is intended.
4. **The GDD says what the two meters' timescales actually are**, or the tuning changes so that
   "minutes-to-hours" becomes true. Either way the belted-at-the-caps case is named explicitly,
   because a player who rigs the safest stance and freezes in thirty seconds will read that as a
   bug unless the design says otherwise.
5. `make check` passes.

## Out of scope
Do not change any value in `meters.json` without a decision recorded here. Do not touch
`MetersNerve.cpp` or `MetersGrip.cpp` — they implement whatever the data says, and both are correct
either way. If question 2 resolves toward changing tuning, that is a separate task with a replay
re-record, not an edit made in passing.
