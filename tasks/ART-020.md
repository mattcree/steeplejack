## Goal
One genuinely good character: model, rig, materials and cloth.

## Why
**The single point of failure of the art direction.** Everything else in this game is procedural,
scanned or instanced. The character is none of those, and at photoreal fidelity a mediocre character
next to a scanned brick wall looks *worse* than a stylised one — photoreal has no tolerance for
inconsistency.

## Context
Options, in order of preference:
1. **MetaHuman, customised.** Fast, high quality, native Control Rig integration, and — relevant
   here — a generated face carries no likeness risk.
2. **Commissioned**, roughly £500–2,000 and 3–4 weeks.
3. Marketplace base mesh, heavily retextured. Last resort.

Brief: a Northern English steeplejack in his sixties. Flat cap, collarless shirt, waistcoat or
overalls, heavy boots, leather tool belt, rope over one shoulder.

**Briefed from a description, never from a photograph of a real person.** This task is the most
likely place in the project for rule 16 to be violated, which is why it carries risk R10. The
likeness review is a blocking acceptance criterion, not a formality.

Clothing must move: Chaos Cloth on the jacket and cap. Wind is a mechanic and the character is how
the player sees it.

## Acceptance
1. Readable in silhouette at 4 m and at 400 m.
2. Rigged and driving Control Rig; compatible with CLIMB-005's FBIK setup.
3. Chaos Cloth on jacket and cap, responding to `sj::Weather::WindAt`, disabled beyond 15 m.
4. Within the 60k triangle budget with LODs.
5. **Likeness review passed** against `docs/05-legal/ip-and-likeness.md`, signed off by the project
   lead, with the source of the design recorded in `## Outcome`.
6. Holds up beside the ART-010 brickwork at 1 m — the consistency test.

## Out of scope
No second character. The client, the crowd and the lad are distant, small or offscreen.
No character customisation beyond hats.
